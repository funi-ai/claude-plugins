# Hooks: the first three seconds

The hook is the only part of an ad most viewers see. It has a spoken half (the first line) and a visual half (the first frame and its first movement); plan both. Every example below is a pattern to fill with the user's facts, not copy to paste: a hook that promises what the ad cannot prove is a claim problem (`scripts-and-claims.md`, section 6).

## 1. Rules

- The first word lands at 0.0 s. No greeting, no "hi guys", no brand name, no logo sting.
- 3 to 8 spoken words (`adkit.py check` warns above 10). One thought. It should raise a question the next beat answers.
- The first frame already shows a person mid-action or the product in an unusual state. A static pack shot is an end card, not a hook.
- Mute test: most feed views start silent. The hook line also appears as a caption (composited, never from the video model), and the first frame makes sense without it.
- The hook must be paid off inside the ad. Curiosity that the product beat does not resolve reads as bait, and ad platforms penalise it.
- Pick the family from the facts sheet, not from taste: no sourced number → no proof-first hook; no real before and after → no transformation hook.
- Turkish hooks are written in Turkish from scratch: questions ending in "biliyor musun?", "hiç düşündün mü?", direct "sen" address and everyday idiom work; translated English formulas ("X hack") do not.

## 2. Families

| # | Family | Mechanism | Needs from the facts sheet | Best modes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Problem callout | names an annoyance the viewer has right now | nothing beyond what the product does | presenter, problem-solution |
| 2 | Open question | asks something the viewer cannot answer yet | the answer must be a real feature | presenter, how-to |
| 3 | Withheld picture | shows an effect without its cause, or a detail without the whole | nothing | showcase, unboxing, tv-spot |
| 4 | Demonstration first | the payoff happens in second one | the product really does this, as shown | how-to, showcase |
| 5 | Proof first | leads with a number, a test or a real quote | a sourced fact (F-id) | testimonial, presenter |
| 6 | Myth or contrarian | "you were told X; it is not true" | the correction is a fact, not an opinion about competitors | presenter, how-to |
| 7 | Audience call | names who this is for | the segment is real for this product | presenter |
| 8 | Objection first | says the viewer's doubt out loud, then answers it | the answer is sourced (price, time, effort) | presenter, problem-solution |
| 9 | Transformation tease | flashes the after, then rewinds | the user's real before and after of the same subject | problem-solution, how-to |
| 10 | Restraint | near-still premium image, one small movement | nothing | tv-spot, showcase |

### 1. Problem callout

- Spoken: "Your iced coffee is warm by ten." · "Kahven onda ılıdı, değil mi?" · "Her salata aynı tat, biliyorum."
- Visual: the annoyance itself in a generic, unbranded setting (a sweating plastic cup, a limp salad), or the presenter mid-sigh holding the old solution out of frame.
- Avoid: statistics about how many people suffer; shaming the viewer's body, skin, age or income.

### 2. Open question

- Spoken: "Why does restaurant salad taste different?" · "Salatanın tadı neden hep aynı, biliyor musun?" · "Guess how long this stays cold."
- Visual: presenter leaning in, eyebrows up, product half in frame; or a close detail that poses the question.
- The PRODUCT or PROOF beat must answer it in plain words. A question about a number needs that number sourced.

### 3. Withheld picture

- Spoken (optional): none, or one word ("Wait."). Music or one clean sound carries it.
- Visual: macro on a material, a stream of oil entering frame from above, hands lifting a lid with the contents still dark, the product as a silhouette against light. The reveal lands at 2 to 4 s, in the next shot or as the end frame of a first-and-last-frame clip.
- Keyframe note: the product may be partly hidden, but whatever is visible must match the photo: crop or shadow it, never blur the label into different letters.

### 4. Demonstration first

- Spoken: "Watch this." · "Şuna bak." · "One pump. That's it."
- Visual: the single most satisfying action of the product in close-up: the pour, the click, the foam, the wipe that leaves a clean line. One action per clip, slow push-in, close sound.
- Claim check: the demonstration shows what the product really does at real speed and quantity. A generated result that exaggerates (stain gone in one wipe, wrinkles gone) is a false claim even without words.

### 5. Proof first

- Spoken: "Twenty-four hours. Still cold." [F-id] · "Dört bin yorumda aynı cümle." [only with the user's number and platform] · a real quote's strongest half-sentence in quotation marks on screen.
- Visual: the number as composited text over the product; a test set-up (thermometer, timer) only if the user ran that test.
- No source → choose another family. Never a star row, badge or "as seen in" strip without platform, date and the user's confirmation.

### 6. Myth or contrarian

- Spoken: "Olive oil shouldn't be cooked? Not quite." · "Pahalı olan iyidir sanıyorsun." · "Stop rinsing it off."
- Visual: presenter shaking the head, or the "wrong way" shown generically for one second, then the turn.
- The correction is about the category or a habit, never a named competitor. Health and nutrition myths need the user's substantiation like any health claim.

### 7. Audience call

- Spoken: "If you meal-prep on Sundays, this one's for you." · "Ofise her gün matara taşıyanlar:" · "New parents, thirty seconds."
- Visual: the segment's world in the first frame (the desk, the stroller, the gym bag).
- Call people by what they do, not by protected or sensitive traits (health conditions, body size, debts, religion, ethnicity): ad platforms reject personal-attribute targeting in copy.

### 8. Objection first

- Spoken: "Yes, it's forty dollars. Here's why." [price from the facts sheet] · "Bir zeytinyağına bu para verilir mi? Dinle." · "I thought it was a gimmick."
- Visual: presenter with a sceptical look holding the product away from the body, then bringing it close on the turn.
- "I thought ..." in the first person is an invented experience for a generated presenter: use the second person ("You think it's a gimmick.") unless a real person speaks their real view.

### 9. Transformation tease

- Spoken: "This was the same pan, ten minutes earlier." · "Aynı tezgâh. On dakika önce."
- Visual: one second of the after, a hard cut to the before, then the story. Both states are the user's real photos or footage; generated clips only animate them gently.
- Not available for body, skin, hair, teeth or weight results unless the user supplies real, unretouched material and confirms the market allows it; even then platforms often reject it.

### 10. Restraint

- Spoken: none for 2 to 3 s, then a calm voice-over line.
- Visual: a large quiet frame, the product small or a macro of its surface, light moving slowly across it; one barely visible camera move.
- Works only when everything is clean: this family exposes every artefact. Use the quality tier and approve the keyframe at full size.

## 3. Visual hook moves that image-to-video handles well

| Move | Prompt words | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Lift into frame | "she lifts the bottle into frame from below, label toward the camera" | 1 to 1.5 s |
| Snap to lens | "she turns from the counter to the camera mid-action and starts talking" | under 1 s |
| Pour or drop entering from above | "a thin stream enters from the top of the frame and lands on ..." | 1.5 to 2 s |
| Light sweep | "a band of light travels across the label from left to right" | 2 s |
| Push-in on detail | "slow push-in on the cap, shallow depth of field" | 2 to 3 s |
| Lid reveal | first-and-last-frame clip: closed box → open box | 2 to 3 s |

Moves that fail often: fast whip pans, text or numbers animating, hands doing fine work (threading, typing), two people interacting, mirrors.

## 4. Hook variants for A/B

- Write three hooks from three different families for the same script body; keep PROBLEM to CTA identical so the test reads cleanly.
- Route B makes this cheap: a new `vo-a` and `head-a` (or a new first b-roll clip) per hook; every other part is reused. Size it with `adkit.py matrix --hooks 3 --visuals 1 --ctas 1 --have-base` (the approved ad is hook 1).
- Name variants by family in the plan (`h1 problem`, `h2 question`, `h3 demo`) so results can be read later.
- A hook that needs a different proof beat is a different ad, not a variant.

## 5. Hooks to refuse or rewrite

| Pattern | Why | Rewrite toward |
|---|---|---|
| "Doctors hate this", "banned in ..." | fabricated authority and conspiracy bait | family 6 with a sourced correction |
| "Only 3 left", countdowns, "today only" without a real limit | fake urgency | family 8 or a plain offer line with the user's real end date |
| "I lost 8 kilos", "I made $5k" from a generated presenter | fabricated testimonial, health or income claim | family 4 or 1 about the product itself |
| "You have acne / debt / a bad back?" | personal-attribute call-out | family 7 by activity, or family 1 about the situation |
| Shock, gore, sexualised framing | platform rejection, brand damage | family 3 or 10 |
| A competitor's name or pack in frame | trademark and comparative-ad risk | generic alternative, sourced difference |
