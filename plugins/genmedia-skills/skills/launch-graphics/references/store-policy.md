# Store and platform policy: what the content may show

What Apple, Google and the launch platforms ask of the **content** of a listing image, what that means for each target mode, and the gates this skill applies on top. Sizes and formats are in `surfaces.md`. **Verified: 2026-09-20** against the pages in section 10, section 2a on 2026-10-07 and again on 2026-10-12; `surfaces.json` and the official page win when they disagree with this file. `launchkit.py` abbreviates the call shown in SKILL.md.

## 1. How to talk about rules

1. Never tell a user that a set "complies", "is approved" or "will pass review". Reviewers decide, rules change, enforcement is uneven. Say what the rule is, what the set does about it, and where the rule lives.
2. A rule that limits a creative choice is stated **once**, in the plan card, as a fact with its source. The user decides. Do not repeat it on every turn and do not refuse a legal choice.
3. Three things are not negotiable here because they mislead buyers: invented proof (section 6), a redrawn interface presented as the real one (section 7), someone else's marks generated by a model (section 8).

## 2. Apple: App Review Guidelines

| Guideline | What it asks | What the skill does |
|---|---|---|
| 2.3 (intro) | metadata, screenshots included, reflects the app's core experience and is kept current | every slide is built on a current capture; an outdated capture is a retake, not a retouch |
| 2.3.3 | screenshots show the app in use, not only title art, a login page or a splash screen; text and image overlays are allowed | at most one `text-only` slide in a set and never as slide 1; slide 1 always shows the app; login and splash captures are rated Retake (`copy-and-narrative.md` section 4) |
| 2.3.7 | screenshots carry no prices or terms that do not belong to that metadata type; no packing with trademarked terms or other apps' names | no price talk on App Store slides; no competitor names in headlines |
| 2.3.10 | no names, icons or imagery of other mobile platforms or alternative marketplaces in the app or its metadata | section 4: nothing Android ever reaches an `appstore-*` target |
| 2.3.1 | marketing an app in a misleading way, inside or outside the store, can remove the app | the claims gate and the `mockup` flag also apply to `og`, `x-card` and `ph-*` targets |
| 5.2.1 | no third-party trademarks or copyrighted material without permission | section 8 |
| 5.6.3 | manipulating reviews, charts or search is not permitted | no invented ratings, review quotes or chart positions, ever |

## 2a. Apple: creative assets (iOS 27 and iPadOS 27)

The product page header, the search results asset and the universal asset (`surfaces.md` section 1a) have their own guidance: Apple's asset best practices, read on 2026-10-07 and again on 2026-10-12, unchanged. Every `appstore-*` creative-asset row in `surfaces.json` lists `content_rules`, and `launchkit.py text` fails each one it finds in that set's copy.

| What Apple asks | Check in `text` (error) | What the skill does beyond that |
|---|---|---|
| no specific prices | `price_in_copy` | a currency amount is an error here, not the screenshot warning |
| no discounts | `discount_in_copy` ("discount", "% off", "coupon", "promo code", "indirim", "kupon", "rabatt"…) | `sale`, `free` and kin still warn as `promo_word`; read every locale yourself |
| no website URLs | `url_in_copy` (a scheme, `www.` or a host with a common top-level domain) | the address belongs in the listing's links, not on the image |
| no copyright symbols | `copyright_symbol` (©, ℗) | - |
| no unverified awards or recognition | the claims gate (section 6) | only what is in `facts.claims` |
| no Apple-designated recognition: Editors' Choice, App of the Day, Game of the Day, Apple Design Award | `apple_badge`, even when the claim is in `facts` | Apple asks to leave them out of these assets altogether |
| no logos of, or references to, other platforms or marketplaces | `other_platform` ("Android", "Google Play", "Play Store", "Galaxy Store", "AppGallery", "Amazon Appstore", "Microsoft Store") | as section 4 rule 1; no other store's badge ever |
| 4+ age suitability whatever the app's rating; no explicit or harmful imagery, gore, weapons aimed at people | none: a word list cannot judge a picture | look at every plate and capture; a capture that shows mature content stays out of these assets |
| text optional, short phrases that add to the picture, localised per language | the layout budgets; one file per locale | offer the text-free version first; the header carries one idea, the search asset says what the app does |
| inclusive imagery for a global audience; current UI | none | a plate prompt names no one's likeness (section 8); captures come from the current version |

The screenshots keep App Review 2.3: these rules are not checked on `appstore-iphone-*`, `appstore-ipad-13` or `appstore-mac`, where section 2 applies.

## 3. Google Play: preview asset guidance

Play's help page separates hard requirements (size, format, counts: `surfaces.md`) from "highly recommended" guidance. The guidance decides eligibility for Play's promotional formats, so treat it as the default.

| Guidance | Detail | What the skill does |
|---|---|---|
| Real experience | screenshots demonstrate the actual in-app experience from captured footage | real captures only; `mockup` flag otherwise |
| Taglines | only when needed, and covering at most 20 percent of the image | `compose` measures the text share on `play-*` targets and warns with `play_tagline_share`; shorten the copy or drop the subline |
| Notification bar | no carrier names, no notifications; battery, Wi-Fi and cell icons full | the status-bar policy repaints or overlays a clean Android bar (9:30, full glyphs; the artwork is from Julien Fovelle's Assets Kit UI, CC BY 4.0, and its credit travels in `proof.json` and belongs in the delivery note: `devices-and-statusbar.md` section 7); a bar it cannot clean is kept with the warning `statusbar_kept_dirty` and a retake tip |
| Content to avoid | device imagery (Google's reason: it dates quickly and can put some users off), third-party characters or logos without permission, the badge or icon of Google Play or any other store | section 4 for the bezel; section 8 for logos; store badges are never added |
| People and devices | no people interacting with the device, such as fingers tapping, unless the app is used off-device | a hand-held hero scene is a link-card and Product Hunt device: `card-hero-scene` renders on card targets only, and on `play-feature` `compose` warns `hero_scene_on_play`. Say the line once and offer `card-left-title` or `card-minimal` |
| Performance and price words | nothing that suggests ranking, accolades, testimonials, price or promotion. Play's examples: "Best", "#1", "Top", "New", "Discount", "Sale", "Million Downloads"; the feature-graphic list adds "Free" | section 6; on Play even a true award stays off the image |
| Calls to action | avoid "Download now", "Install now", "Play now", "Try now" | no call-to-action copy on store targets |
| Small or crowded text | not visible on many phones | budgets in `copy-and-narrative.md`; `chars_per_line` warning |
| Time-sensitive content | avoid, or swap it out on time | a seasonal set gets an end date in the delivery note |
| Split interface | stylised screenshots that break the interface across several images are allowed; keep the interface prominent in the first three | panoramic `strip` sets keep a full device on slides 1 to 3; a `device-span` pair among them warns `span_early` (Google's wording re-read on 2026-09-23: "prioritize UI in the first three screenshots") |
| Large screens | leave out text that is not part of the app | `play-tablet` slides: shortest copy of the set |
| Feature graphic | conveys the experience, does not repeat the icon's branding, focal point centred, brand logo, app name, primary slogan and main UI outside the cutoff zones, background elements restricted to the edges, no pure white, black or dark grey ground, no device imagery, localised where it carries text. Google states that failing the focal point and the cutoff zones may leave the app ineligible for the formats that add their own UI overlays | `play-feature` takes a card layout, laid out inside the safe box (about 15 percent per edge, `surfaces.md` section 1); `compose` warns `cutoff_zone` when text, the lockup or a capture still reaches a zone. `frame: "auto"` draws the Android phone there, so to follow Google's line choose `card-minimal`, or `frame: "none"` for a frameless capture, and say which you chose |
| Device Art Generator | Google's own framing tool says its output is not for the feature image or screenshots of a Play listing and recommends the bare screenshot | one more reason the frame is a disclosed choice on Play |

## 4. Which frame goes where, and what `universal` must not show

`frame: "auto"` resolves per target: `appstore-iphone-*` → `iphone`, `appstore-ipad-13` → `ipad`, `appstore-mac` → `macos-window`, `play-phone` → `android-phone`, `play-tablet` and the two tablet slots → `android-tablet`, whatever the capture's `os` is. On cards a `web` or `desktop` capture gets the `browser` window, `play-feature` gets the Android phone, the App Store creative assets (`appstore-header`, `appstore-search`, `appstore-universal`) the iPhone, and other cards follow the capture's `os`. The full rules are in `templates.md` section 3.

| Mode | Frames and bars | Say once in the plan card |
|---|---|---|
| `app-store` | iPhone-style and iPad-style frames drawn by this skill, iOS bar at 9:41 with full glyphs | that the frames are this project's drawings, not Apple's product bezels, and what Apple's marketing guidelines say about that (section 5) |
| `play` | Pixel-class Android frame by default, Android bar at 9:30 with full glyphs | "Google lists device imagery under content to avoid; `frame: none` gives a frameless set. Which do you prefer?" as an offer, with the framed set as the default |
| `both` | two sets, same copy and layout choices, each store with its own frame, bar and, where the interface differs, its own captures | that the Play set uses Android captures when the user has them |
| `universal` | `neutral-phone` or `none`; status strip without glyphs | that one design serves both stores and therefore shows no platform at all |

Rules that follow from 2.3.10 and hold in every mode:

1. An `android-phone` or `android-tablet` frame, a punch-hole cutout, Android status glyphs, a three-button or gesture navigation bar, a Material system dialog, the words "Android" or "Google Play" and any store badge never appear on an `appstore-*` target. A capture with `os: "android"` that shows system UI is not used there; ask for the iOS capture. The script only helps with the bar: a flat foreign status bar is repainted with the target platform's own, any other is kept with the warning `statusbar_os_mismatch`. This holds with `frame: "none"` too, in both directions — a frameless capture takes the bars of the target's store, never of its own OS, so an Android bar cannot reach an `appstore-*` target and an iOS bar cannot reach a Play one without that warning.
2. A `universal` deck carries nothing that identifies either platform: no island, no punch-hole, no glyphs, no system share sheet, no store names in copy. An iOS or Android frame named in a universal store set fails deck validation (`universal_frame`). A capture whose own status bar cannot be blanked stays and `compose` warns `statusbar_identifies_os`: tell the user and ask for a clean capture or for `both`.
3. Play's page names no rule about another platform's device. An iPhone frame on a Play listing still misstates what the buyer installs, and device imagery is on Play's avoid list anyway: do not do it by default.
4. References to the other platform belong in words on the user's own site ("also on Android"), never in a store image.

## 5. Apple marketing guidelines: what to tell the user

Apple's marketing guidelines cover how Apple products are shown in marketing material. They are a separate document from the App Review Guidelines and stricter about devices; this skill does not claim to know how either is enforced. So this is **a fact to tell the user, not a prohibition**.

- Product images are the Apple-provided bezels, used "as is": no added reflections, shadows or highlights, no cropping, tilting or covering, no flipping or animating, no 3D render or simulation of an Apple product. Copy goes beside the device, not on top of it.
- Show the latest-generation device the app is built for, on the latest OS. The status bar shows a full network icon, full Wi-Fi and a full battery; a full-screen app may extend over the bar.
- The screen shows the app as it runs, never blank, with fictional account data instead of a real person's.
- Apple products appear on their own: no competing devices next to them. Other platforms are mentioned only in copy or with badges.
- Custom photography is allowed when people use the product in an authentic way; straight-on shots are preferred, extreme angles are not.

What it means here. Nothing in the deck, the proof or the manifest records this: there is no such field. It is one sentence you say in the plan card. The shipped `iphone` and `ipad` frames are this project's own drawings, so a set that uses them does not follow Apple's product-image rule; neither does a device that bleeds off the canvas (the default of `device-bottom`, `device-top` and `two-devices`), nor a generated hand-held scene. The closest a set gets: an Apple-provided bezel the user imported (`launchkit.py assets import --kind bezel`, `devices-and-statusbar.md` section 6) on a layout that shows it whole and upright (`device-center`, or `bleed: false`), with no decor over it and without `bezel_shadow`. By default the compositor adds no shadow, reflection or highlight to an imported bezel (`bezel_shadow: true` on a set or slide adds a drop shadow, which Apple's text counts as a modification). Then you may say what is true, "Apple's own bezel, whole, upright and unmodified", and still never "compliant": reviewers decide. Offer the import path once and carry on with what the user chooses. Apple's bezel files and Apple's UI-kit status bars are never shipped, fetched or redistributed by this skill: the Apple Design Resources licence allows mock-ups for Apple-platform software and rules out redistribution, so they are import-only, from the user's own download.

## 6. The claims gate

`launchkit.py text` enforces it; the agent applies the same rule to every locale, because the script's word list cannot know every language.

1. **Numbers come from `facts.numbers`.** A digit string in copy that is not in the list fails. No rounding up, no "10K+" for 9,400.
2. **Claims come from `facts.claims`.** Ratings, awards, press quotes, user counts, "trusted by", certifications, chart positions: only the user's exact wording, and only when they supplied it. Ask for the source once; keep it in the plan.
3. **Never invented:** reviews, star rows, user counts, testimonials, customer logos, "as seen in" rows, download counters. Not as placeholders either: a placeholder gets shipped.
4. **Superlatives, rankings and calls to action.** The script knows Play's English list (`#1`, `best`, `top`, `free`, `download now`, `install now`, `play now`, `try now`): `banned_store_word` is an error in a set with a `play-*` target and a warning in every other set. Kindred words (`new`, `sale`, `discount`, "en iyi", "1 numara", "ücretsiz", "gratis", "meilleur"…) only warn as `promo_word`, and words the lists do not hold ("lider") pass unseen: read every locale yourself. On store targets such words go; on `og` and `ph-*` they stay only when the user supplied the claim.
5. **Price and promotion** ("free", "ücretsiz", "%50 indirim", "sale") stay off store targets (Apple 2.3.7, Play's guidance); a currency amount in store copy warns as `price_in_copy`. On a link card a price is allowed when it is in `facts.numbers` and the page it links to shows the same price.
6. **True proof still has a place.** App Store: slide 1 only. Play: not on the image; it belongs in the listing text. Link cards and Product Hunt: one data point per card (`og-cards.md` section 2).
7. Comparative claims against a named competitor are refused on every target.

## 7. The `mockup` flag

Any screen that is not an untouched capture of the running product carries `mockup: true` on its entry in `captures`: a design-tool export, a screen of a feature that has not shipped, a capture with edited content. The flag travels into the proof file of every slide that uses it, into `manifest.json` and into the delivery note ("slides 3 and 5 show design mockups, not captures").

- Why: both stores ask for the real experience, and a buyer who installs the app expects the screen they saw. The user may have good reasons (a pre-launch page, a Product Hunt teaser); the flag keeps the choice visible to whoever uploads the files.
- The skill never produces a mockup of its own: it does not redraw, "enhance", relight or upscale the interface with a generative model, and it does not edit words or numbers inside a capture. Wrong data in a capture is a retake.
- A store set where every slide is a mockup: say plainly that guideline 2.3.3 and Play's guidance ask for the app in use, and let the user decide.

## 8. Trademarks, third-party logos, people and data

- Integration and partner logos come **from files the user supplies**, used unmodified, and only when the user confirms they may show them. A model never draws, completes or "cleans up" a third party's mark, and a plate prompt never names a brand.
- No store badges on store targets (Play lists them under content to avoid; Apple's badge rules are for marketing material outside the store). On a link card a badge is the user's file, unmodified.
- Generated scene art shows a generic handset. Do not prompt for a named phone model, a logo on the back, or another maker's device next to it.
- Captures show fictional or the user's own demo data. Real names, e-mail addresses, faces or account numbers of other people in a capture → Retake, with the reason.
- Generated people in plates are fictional and unnamed; no likeness of a real or famous person.

## 9. What this skill does not do

It does not upload to App Store Connect, the Play Console, Product Hunt or any site, does not ask for or handle store credentials, does not submit for review, does not write the listing text, keywords or alt text fields into a console, and does not predict a review outcome. It delivers files in order, a manifest, and a note of every flag raised above.
