# Hooks: the first two seconds

The hook is designed before anything else and written as beat 1 of the beat sheet. One video, one hook mechanism. The numbers below are craft heuristics that give a starting point; none of them is a platform rule.

## Rules for every hook

1. **One sentence.** If a stranger cannot picture beat 1 from a single sentence, it is not a hook yet.
2. **Inside 1-2 s.** The mechanism is visible before 2.0 s (before 1.5 s when the placement is a fast feed). Nothing warms up: no logo sting, no title card, no greeting, no fade from black.
3. **Sound-off test.** The hook must work muted: a shape, a motion or 3-5 composited words carry it. Sound then doubles it, it never replaces it.
4. **It makes a promise the video keeps.** The payoff answers the question the hook raised, within the same video. A hook the body cannot pay off is a different video.
5. **Aggressive camera lives here only.** Snap, whip and drop moves are allowed in beat 1 and nowhere else (`craft.md`).
6. **The first frame is a poster.** Platforms may show frame 1 as a preview: it must be sharp, lit and composed, never black or mid-blur.

## Spoken hooks

- First word at 0.0 s. No greeting, no name, no "in this video".
- 3-8 words, one breath. A claim that raises a question ("Cold brew is not iced coffee."), a number the user supplied, or a direct "you" statement.
- The spoken hook and the text hook say the same thing in different words, or the text is a 3-5 word compression of the line. Never two different messages.
- Turkish and other languages: keep the hook in the publishing language; do not translate a punchy English line word by word, rewrite it for rhythm.

## Text hooks (composited, never generated)

- 3-5 words, on screen from the first frame, off by the end of beat 1 or 2 (reading time = words / 3 s, minimum 1.2 s).
- Inside the safe zone of `platforms.md`, on the clean area the keyframe prompt reserved for it.
- One weight, one colour, high contrast. No second line unless it is the brand handle.
- F10: the quotable sentence itself, up to 12 words, held about 1.5 s while the audio is already running.

## Sound hooks

| Pattern | How |
|---|---|
| silence, then one hit | near-silence for 1.0-1.5 s, a low hit on the cut or the break |
| pure foley | close-miked material sound, no music until beat 2 |
| voice at frame one | the spoken hook alone, music enters at 1-2 s |
| tone that stops | a rising tone that ends dead on the reveal |

Music normally enters after the hook. A hook that depends on a trending sound is the user's job at upload time: deliver a version that works without it.

## Re-hook (30 s and longer)

Attention sags near the middle. Plant one beat there that restarts curiosity: a question in the voice, a second reveal, a change of place or scale, a counter-claim. It is a normal beat (2-4 s, one move), marked `re-hook` in the beat sheet.

## Banned hooks

- Strobing, rapid flashing or high-contrast flicker: nothing that flashes more than three times within one second (the common accessibility threshold for photosensitive viewers), and no large saturated-red flashes at all.
- Fake platform interface: fake notifications, fake incoming calls, fake "live" badges, fake news lower thirds.
- Invented proof: numbers, ratings, customer counts, "as seen on" marks the user did not supply.
- A real person's face, voice or lookalike without the user's confirmation of consent; public figures never.
- Shock content, injuries, or a claim the video does not keep (bait and switch).
- Text that tells the viewer to wait ("wait for it", "watch till the end") instead of giving a reason to.
